## Mission Overview

Starship Flight 15 was a landmark test of SpaceX's fully reusable launch system. It demonstrated that an orbital Starship upper stage could return to its launch site and be caught by the launch tower.

## Launch and Ascent

<figure>
  <img src="https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/VeeqQAV9pGgQvCWQTrA9CT.jpg"
       alt="Starship at Starbase before Flight 15">
  <figcaption>Starship at Starbase ahead of Flight 15. Image credit: SpaceX / Space.com.</figcaption>
</figure>

Starship lifted off from Starbase, Texas, on 13 November 2026 at approximately 4:47 p.m. Central Time.

The Super Heavy booster powered the initial ascent. Following maximum aerodynamic pressure, the vehicle approached stage separation.

### Hot staging

At approximately T+2 minutes 21 seconds, Starship initiated hot staging. The upper-stage engines ignited while the stages separated, allowing Ship 42 to continue its journey towards orbit.

Ship 42 subsequently completed its ascent and orbital insertion burn.

## Booster 22: Recovery Attempt

<figure>
  <img src="https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/i6Nx8sCoBwGy99pEqF4djm.jpg"
       alt="Super Heavy booster during a flight test">
  <figcaption>Super Heavy booster flight-test imagery. Image credit: Space.com / Josh Dinner.</figcaption>
</figure>

Booster 22 successfully performed its primary launch function and separated from the upper stage.

However, its planned return to the Starbase tower did not succeed.

- The booster performed its flip and boostback sequence.
- The boostback burn ended earlier than planned.
- Guidance detected that the booster was not in the required position for a safe tower catch.
- The autonomous system aborted the catch attempt.
- The booster diverted toward the Gulf of Mexico.
- The booster was lost before completing recovery.

<div class="callout-note">
  <strong>Investigation:</strong> SpaceX reported early indications of a low-fuel-mass alarm associated with the shortened boostback burn. The definitive root cause remained under investigation.
</div>

## Ship 42: Orbital Operations

Following separation, Ship 42 continued its powered ascent and reached low Earth orbit at approximately 275 kilometres altitude.

The spacecraft remained in orbit for approximately 22.5 hours and completed around 14 revolutions of Earth.

Mission objectives included testing propulsion, navigation, electrical power, communications, orbital operations and preparation for atmospheric return.

## Deployment of 26 Starlink V3 Satellites

One of the mission's major commercial achievements was the successful deployment of 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites.

## Heat Shield Technology

<figure>
  <img src="https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/wPeeKCdF2RvbiNJiDsUjER.jpg"
       alt="Starship Ship 42 thermal protection tiles">
  <figcaption>Ship 42 and its thermal protection system. Image credit: SpaceX / Space.com.</figcaption>
</figure>

Atmospheric reentry exposes Starship to extreme temperatures. Its thermal protection system must prevent excessive heating of the spacecraft structure.

Flight 15 incorporated improvements developed from previous testing, including:

- Additional tile-retention mechanisms in vulnerable areas.
- Changes addressing potential plasma flow behind tiles.
- Greater use of curved heat-shield tiles.
- Efforts to reduce heating in tile gaps.

SpaceX assessed heat-shield condition using imagery and inspection data before authorising the return attempt. Ship 42 subsequently completed reentry and the tower catch.

## Historic Orbital Starship Tower Catch

<figure>
  <img src="https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/VeeqQAV9pGgQvCWQTrA9CT.jpg"
       alt="Starship at the Starbase launch tower">
  <figcaption>Starship tower operations at Starbase. Image credit: SpaceX / Space.com.</figcaption>
</figure>

**The defining achievement of Flight 15 was the successful recovery of Ship 42 at Starbase.**

After approximately 22.5 hours in space, Ship 42 performed its deorbit burn, reentered the atmosphere and returned to the launch site.

### How the catch worked

1. Ship 42 performed a deorbit burn.
2. The spacecraft entered Earth's atmosphere.
3. Its heat shield protected it during reentry.
4. Aerodynamic control surfaces guided its descent.
5. The spacecraft transitioned to a vertical orientation.
6. Its engines performed the final landing manoeuvre.
7. The launch tower's mechanical arms captured the vehicle.

<div class="callout-note callout-highlight">
  <strong>Historic first:</strong> Flight 15 achieved the first successful tower catch of a Starship upper stage returning from orbit.
</div>

## Why Flight 15 Matters

### Reusable launch systems

The successful orbital return and tower catch represent a major step towards recovering both stages of Starship.

### Commercial satellite deployment

Starship's payload capacity could enable much larger satellite deployments and future space infrastructure.

### NASA's Artemis programme

Starship is being developed as a human landing system for NASA's lunar exploration programme. Orbital refuelling, reliability and crew safety remain critical milestones.

### Future Mars exploration

SpaceX ultimately envisions Starship transporting cargo and people to Mars. Reusability, orbital operations and reliable recovery are important prerequisites for that ambition.

### Challenges ahead

- Investigate Booster 22's recovery failure.
- Demonstrate repeatable booster and ship catches.
- Reduce inspection and refurbishment requirements.
- Demonstrate in-space propellant transfer.
- Validate long-duration operations and crew safety.
